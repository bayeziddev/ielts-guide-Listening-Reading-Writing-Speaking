# 2. Noun

> **Chapter focus:** countability, articles, plurals, possession, and word formation.

## How to study this lesson

Read the explanation first, then say the examples aloud. Change one detail in each example, such as the subject, time, or place. Complete the practice without looking back and use the answer key to understand the reason for each answer. Finish by writing two sentences about your own life or city.

A **noun** names a person, place, object, substance, activity, or idea. IELTS requires careful control of noun number, countability, articles, and word formation.

### Common noun types

A **common noun** refers to a general class (*city, student*); a **proper noun** names a particular person or place (*London, Asia*). A **concrete noun** can be experienced physically (*road, water*), while an **abstract noun** refers to an idea or quality (*development, equality*). A **collective noun** refers to a group (*team, government, population*).

### Countable and uncountable nouns

Countable nouns have singular and plural forms: *one idea, two ideas*. A singular countable noun normally needs a determiner: **A student needs feedback**, not **Student needs feedback**. Uncountable nouns are treated as substances, categories, or concepts: *information, advice, research, equipment, traffic, accommodation,* and *progress*. They do not normally take *-s*.

Use **much/little** with uncountable nouns and **many/few** with countable nouns. Use **a piece of advice**, **a piece of information**, or **an item of equipment** when you need a countable unit.

### Articles and possession

Use **a/an** for one non-specific countable thing: **A solution is needed**. Use **the** when the reader knows which thing you mean or when the noun is unique in the context: **The solution proposed by the committee was expensive**. Use no article for plural or uncountable nouns when speaking generally: **Public parks improve quality of life** and **Education is important**.

For possession, use an apostrophe: **the government’s policy**. For a plural ending in *-s*, place the apostrophe after the *s*: **students’ results**. Do not confuse **its** (possessive) with **it’s** (it is).

### IELTS word-formation warning

A noun may require a different suffix: *develop → development*, *accurate → accuracy*, *responsible → responsibility*. Spelling changes are common. In a summary-completion task, check whether the gap needs a noun and whether the answer must be singular or plural.

### Practice

Correct these sentences: (a) The government should provide more informations. (b) Student need access to library. (c) The peoples’ opinions were divided.

**Answers:** (a) The government should provide more **information**. (b) **Students need access to a library** or **A student needs access to the library**, depending on the meaning. (c) **The people’s opinions were divided**; *people* is already plural.
