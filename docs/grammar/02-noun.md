# 2. Noun

> **Chapter focus:** countability, articles, plurals, possession, and word formation.

## How to study this lesson

Read the explanation first, then say the examples aloud. Change one detail in each example, such as the subject, time, or place. Complete the practice without looking back and use the answer key to understand the reason for each answer. Finish by writing two sentences about your own life or city.

A **noun** names a person, place, object, substance, activity, or idea. IELTS requires careful control of noun number, countability, articles, and word formation.

### Common noun types

A **common noun** refers to a general class (*city, student*); a **proper noun** names a particular person or place (*London, Asia*). A **concrete noun** can be experienced physically (*road, water*), while an **abstract noun** refers to an idea or quality (*development, equality*). A **collective noun** refers to a group (*team, government, population*).

### Countable and uncountable nouns

Countable nouns have singular and plural forms: *one idea, two ideas*. A singular countable noun normally needs a determiner: **A student needs feedback**, not **Student needs feedback**. Uncountable nouns are treated as substances, categories, or concepts: *information, advice, research, equipment, traffic, accommodation,* and *progress*. They do not normally take *-s*.

Use **much/little** with uncountable nouns and **many/few** with countable nouns. Use **a piece of advice**, **a piece of information**, or **an item of equipment** when you need a countable unit.

### Articles and possession

Use **a/an** for one non-specific countable thing: **A solution is needed**. Use **the** when the reader knows which thing you mean or when the noun is unique in the context: **The solution proposed by the committee was expensive**. Use no article for plural or uncountable nouns when speaking generally: **Public parks improve quality of life** and **Education is important**.

For possession, use an apostrophe: **the government’s policy**. For a plural ending in *-s*, place the apostrophe after the *s*: **students’ results**. Do not confuse **its** (possessive) with **it’s** (it is).

### IELTS word-formation warning

A noun may require a different suffix: *develop → development*, *accurate → accuracy*, *responsible → responsibility*. Spelling changes are common. In a summary-completion task, check whether the gap needs a noun and whether the answer must be singular or plural.

### Practice

Correct these sentences: (a) The government should provide more informations. (b) Student need access to library. (c) The peoples’ opinions were divided.

**Answers:** (a) The government should provide more **information**. (b) **Students need access to a library** or **A student needs access to the library**, depending on the meaning. (c) **The people’s opinions were divided**; *people* is already plural.

## Deep lesson: countability, articles, and precise nouns

The most important noun question is not “What is the Bengali meaning?” It is **Can I count this noun as one item?** A countable noun can be singular or plural: **one solution, two solutions**. An uncountable noun names a substance, field, activity, or general idea: **information, advice, research, pollution**.

### Article decision tree

Use **a/an** when you mean one non-specific countable thing: **A solution is needed.** Use **the** when both reader and writer can identify the thing: **The solution proposed by the committee is expensive.** Use no article for a general plural or general uncountable idea: **Solutions require funding. Education matters.**

| Meaning | Correct pattern | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| one of many | a/an + singular countable noun | **a university** |
| known or specified | the + noun | **the university near my home** |
| general plural | plural noun | **universities need funding** |
| general concept | uncountable noun | **higher education needs funding** |

### Twelve examples

1. **A student** needs feedback.
2. **Students** need feedback.
3. **The students in this class** need feedback.
4. **Advice** is useful; **a piece of advice** is one item.
5. **Information** is available; **a piece of information** is one fact.
6. **Research** takes time; **a study** is one research project.
7. **The research published in 2025** was influential.
8. **Traffic** is uncountable; say **heavy traffic**, not *many traffics*.
9. **The traffic on this road** is dangerous.
10. **People** is already plural; do not write *peoples* for ordinary people.
11. **Children** is the plural of **child**, not *childs*.
12. **The government’s policy** shows possession; **governments’ policies** refers to several governments.

### Bengali-speaker errors

Bengali often allows a noun without an article where English requires one. **Wrong:** Student should read book. **Right:** **A student should read a book** (general individual) or **Students should read books** (general group). Another common error is adding *-s* to uncountable nouns. **Wrong:** many advices and informations. **Right:** **much advice and information**.

### IELTS application

In Task 1, noun precision prevents vague descriptions: **the percentage of households**, **a sharp increase**, **the number of visitors**. In Task 2, use abstract nouns carefully: **access to education**, **economic development**, **environmental protection**. Remember the preposition as part of the noun phrase.

### Practice

A. Correct: **The government should give informations to citizens.** Answer: **The government should give information to citizens** or **a piece of information**.

B. Add articles: **___ university introduced ___ environmental course.** Answer: **A university introduced an environmental course** if both are new and non-specific.

C. Choose: **The number/amount of students is rising.** Answer: **number**, because students are countable. **The amount of traffic is rising** because traffic is uncountable.

## Professor's explanation: how to think about this grammar

Do not begin by memorising a definition. Begin by asking three questions: **What job does this form do? What meaning does it add? Where does it go in the sentence?** Bengali and English often arrange information differently, so direct translation can create mistakes. Build the English pattern first, then attach your idea to it.

## Bengali learner warning

Many Bengali speakers understand the meaning of an English sentence but omit a small grammar word because Bengali does not always need the same word. English normally needs a clear subject and a finite verb. For example, say **The number of students is increasing**, not **Number of students increasing**. Treat articles, auxiliary verbs, plural endings, and prepositions as part of the pattern, not as optional decoration.

## Three-step study method

**Step 1 — Notice:** underline the target form in every example. **Step 2 — Control:** replace only one part of the example, such as the subject or time. **Step 3 — Communicate:** use the form in a short answer about education, health, transport, technology, or the environment. If you cannot explain why you chose the form, return to Step 1.

## Practice ladder

**Level 1: Recognition.** Find the target form and name its job. **Level 2: Controlled production.** Complete or correct a sentence using the pattern. **Level 3: IELTS production.** Write a short paragraph or speak for one minute. At Level 3, clarity is more important than using many different forms.

## Revision checklist

Before moving on, ask: Can I explain the form in one simple sentence? Can I make a positive, negative, and question form where relevant? Can I use it with a past and a present example? Can I recognise the most common Bengali-speaker error? Can I use it naturally in one IELTS answer?
