# 1. Introduction to Parts of Speech

> **Chapter focus:** parts of speech, word families, and grammar labels.

## How to study this lesson

Read the explanation first, then say the examples aloud. Change one detail in each example, such as the subject, time, or place. Complete the practice without looking back and use the answer key to understand the reason for each answer. Finish by writing two sentences about your own life or city.

A **part of speech** is a group of words classified by the job it performs in a sentence. Traditional grammar commonly identifies nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions, and interjections. A word can belong to different classes in different sentences.

### The main word classes

| Word class | Main job | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Noun | names a person, place, thing, or idea | *student, Dhaka, pollution, freedom* |
| Pronoun | replaces a noun | *she, they, which, someone* |
| Verb | expresses an action, event, or state | *study, increase, seem* |
| Adjective | describes a noun or pronoun | *large, useful, environmental* |
| Adverb | modifies a verb, adjective, adverb, or whole clause | *carefully, very, fortunately* |
| Preposition | shows a relationship, often of time, place, or direction | *in, at, from, because of* |
| Conjunction | connects words, phrases, or clauses | *and, but, although* |
| Interjection | expresses a brief feeling or reaction | *Oh! Wow!* |

Consider the sentence: **The highly motivated students completed the difficult task carefully because the deadline was close.** *Students* and *task* are nouns; *completed* and *was* are verbs; *motivated* and *difficult* are adjectives; *highly* and *carefully* are adverbs; *because* is a conjunction; and *the* is a determiner. Determiners such as *a, an, the, this,* and *many* are also important even though they are often taught separately.

### Word families for IELTS

Academic English frequently changes one root word into several forms: *educate* (verb), *education* (noun), *educational* (adjective), and *educationally* (adverb). Build vocabulary in families, but check the spelling and the grammar of each form. In a Task 1 sentence, write **The percentage increased**, not **The percentage increase** when a verb is required.

### Practice

Identify the bold word class: (a) The **rapid** growth created pressure. (b) People **adapt** to change. (c) The policy was implemented **gradually**. (d) **Although** the plan was expensive, it was effective.

**Answers:** (a) adjective, (b) verb, (c) adverb, (d) conjunction.

## Deep lesson: word jobs, not word labels

A word class tells you the job a word is doing **in this sentence**. The same spelling can change class. In **I work every day**, *work* is a verb. In **I have a lot of work**, *work* is a noun. This is why you should analyse the sentence, not memorise a word as permanently belonging to one class.

| Job | Question to ask | Example | Bengali support |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Noun | Who or what? | **Education** changes lives. | নাম/ধারণা |
| Verb | What happens or exists? | Education **changes** lives. | কাজ/অবস্থা |
| Adjective | Which one or what kind? | **Public** education matters. | গুণ/বর্ণনা |
| Adverb | How, when, where, or how much? | It changes lives **rapidly**. | কাজের ধরন |
| Pronoun | Which noun is replaced? | **It** changes lives. | সর্বনাম |
| Preposition | What relationship? | access **to** education | সম্পর্ক |
| Conjunction | How are ideas joined? | Education matters **because** it creates opportunity. | সংযোগ |

### Twelve examples to study

1. **Students** (noun) **learn** (verb) **quickly** (adverb).
2. **The** (determiner) **new** (adjective) library opened yesterday.
3. **They** (pronoun) use it **for** (preposition) research.
4. The programme is **extremely** useful.
5. **Although** the course is demanding, it is practical.
6. **Research** can be a noun: Researchers **research** problems.
7. **Increase** is a noun in **an increase**, but a verb in **prices increase**.
8. **Online** can describe a noun in **online classes** or an activity in **study online**.
9. The **rapid** (adjective) **growth** (noun) affected housing.
10. The city acted **responsibly** (adverb).
11. **Wow!** is an interjection, but it is unsuitable for a formal essay.
12. **To reduce** pollution, governments need **effective** policies.

### Word-family ladder

Build four sentences from one root: **educate** (verb), **education** (noun), **educational** (adjective), **educationally** (adverb). Example: **Schools educate children. Education improves opportunity. Educational resources must be affordable. The policy is educationally valuable.** Do not assume every *-ly* word is an adverb or every *-tion* word is spelled predictably; check the form in a dictionary.

### Common errors and corrections

**Wrong:** The government should provide free educate. **Right:** The government should provide free **education**.
**Wrong:** People can easy access the service. **Right:** People can access the service **easily**.
**Wrong:** Many student are benefit from it. **Right:** Many **students benefit** from it.
**Wrong:** The city made a rapidly improvement. **Right:** The city made a **rapid improvement**.

### Practice with reasons

A. Label the bold word: **The policy significantly reduced traffic.** Answer: *policy* is a noun, *significantly* is an adverb, *reduced* is a verb, and *traffic* is an uncountable noun.

B. Choose: **The government made a (decide/decision) to (improve/improvement) transport.** Answer: **decision** and **improve**. The article *a* needs a countable noun; *to* needs a base verb.

C. IELTS task: Write three sentences about education. Use one noun, one adjective, one adverb, one connector, and one verb. Check the job of every target word.

## Professor's explanation: how to think about this grammar

Do not begin by memorising a definition. Begin by asking three questions: **What job does this form do? What meaning does it add? Where does it go in the sentence?** Bengali and English often arrange information differently, so direct translation can create mistakes. Build the English pattern first, then attach your idea to it.

## Bengali learner warning

Many Bengali speakers understand the meaning of an English sentence but omit a small grammar word because Bengali does not always need the same word. English normally needs a clear subject and a finite verb. For example, say **The number of students is increasing**, not **Number of students increasing**. Treat articles, auxiliary verbs, plural endings, and prepositions as part of the pattern, not as optional decoration.

## Three-step study method

**Step 1 — Notice:** underline the target form in every example. **Step 2 — Control:** replace only one part of the example, such as the subject or time. **Step 3 — Communicate:** use the form in a short answer about education, health, transport, technology, or the environment. If you cannot explain why you chose the form, return to Step 1.

## Practice ladder

**Level 1: Recognition.** Find the target form and name its job. **Level 2: Controlled production.** Complete or correct a sentence using the pattern. **Level 3: IELTS production.** Write a short paragraph or speak for one minute. At Level 3, clarity is more important than using many different forms.

## Revision checklist

Before moving on, ask: Can I explain the form in one simple sentence? Can I make a positive, negative, and question form where relevant? Can I use it with a past and a present example? Can I recognise the most common Bengali-speaker error? Can I use it naturally in one IELTS answer?
