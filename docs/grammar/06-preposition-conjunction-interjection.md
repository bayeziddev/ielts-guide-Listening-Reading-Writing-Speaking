# 6. Preposition, Conjunction and Interjection

> **Chapter focus:** preposition patterns, clause connectors, punctuation, and register.

## How to study this lesson

Read the explanation first, then say the examples aloud. Change one detail in each example, such as the subject, time, or place. Complete the practice without looking back and use the answer key to understand the reason for each answer. Finish by writing two sentences about your own life or city.

A **preposition** introduces a noun phrase and shows a relationship. Common relationships include place (*in a city, on the table, at school*), time (*in 2026, on Monday, at 9 a.m.*), direction (*to the station, from home*), and cause or method (*because of rain, by train*).

### Preposition patterns

Use **interested in**, **responsible for**, **depend on**, **similar to**, **different from**, and **increase in**. Learn the whole phrase rather than translating each preposition from your first language. A preposition is followed by a noun, pronoun, or *-ing* form: **interested in learning**.

Use **during + noun**: **during the lesson**. Use **while + clause**: **while the lesson was taking place**. Use **despite/in spite of + noun or -ing**: **despite the cost** and **despite being expensive**. Use **although/even though + clause**: **Although it was expensive, it was adopted**.

A **coordinating conjunction** joins equal elements: *and, but, or, so, yet, for, nor*. A **subordinating conjunction** introduces a dependent clause: *because, although, if, when, whereas, while, unless*. A **correlative conjunction** works as a pair: *both…and, either…or, neither…nor, not only…but also*.

Use a comma after an introductory dependent clause: **Although the sample was small, the result was useful.** When the independent clause comes first, a comma is often unnecessary: **The result was useful although the sample was small.** Do not join two complete sentences with only a comma. Use a full stop, semicolon, or a conjunction.

**Interjections** such as *Oh!* and *Wow!* are common in informal speech but rarely belong in IELTS Writing. In Speaking, natural reactions are acceptable, but do not rely on fillers such as *you know* to replace ideas.

### Practice

Correct the choices: (a) Despite of the rain, the event continued. (b) Although the method was cheap. It was unreliable. (c) The scheme reduced waste, it also created jobs.

**Answers:** (a) **Despite the rain** or **In spite of the rain**; (b) **Although the method was cheap, it was unreliable**; (c) **The scheme reduced waste and also created jobs** or **The scheme reduced waste; it also created jobs**.
