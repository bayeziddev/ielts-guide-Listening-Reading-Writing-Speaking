# 14. Idioms

> **Chapter focus:** meaning, register, collocations, natural use, and avoiding memorised language.

## How to study this lesson

Read the explanation first, then say the examples aloud. Change one detail in each example, such as the subject, time, or place. Complete the practice without looking back and use the answer key to understand the reason for each answer. Finish by writing two sentences about your own life or city.

An **idiom** is a fixed expression whose meaning is not always predictable from its individual words, such as **a piece of cake** meaning “easy.” Idioms can make informal speech lively, but they are not a shortcut to a high IELTS score.

### Idioms in IELTS

In Speaking, a familiar idiom can sound natural when it fits the topic and your normal way of speaking: **Getting used to university life was a bit of a learning curve.** Do not memorise long lists and insert them into every answer. A forced idiom may sound inaccurate or rehearsed.

In Writing, avoid conversational idioms in formal essays: **The government must bite the bullet** is less appropriate than **The government must take difficult but necessary action**. Some fixed academic expressions are useful, but they are better learned as collocations: **play a role, address an issue, pose a challenge, reach a conclusion, take measures,** and **have a significant impact on**.

### Safe practice method

Learn an idiom with its meaning, register, grammar, and one natural example. Mark it as informal, neutral, or formal. Check whether it is common in British English and whether the context supports it. Never change part of an idiom unless you are certain that the variation exists.

### Useful natural expressions

For Speaking, appropriate expressions include **from my point of view**, **a turning point**, **once in a while**, **to some extent**, and **a learning curve**. For formal Writing, prefer **in the long term**, **a key factor**, **a considerable proportion**, **in terms of**, and **as a result**. These are expressions, not decorations: each must communicate a precise relationship or meaning.

### Practice

Replace the informal expression with a formal alternative: (a) The project cost an arm and a leg. (b) The policy was a game changer. (c) The plan went down the drain.

**Possible answers:** (a) **The project was extremely expensive.** (b) **The policy had a transformative effect.** (c) **The plan failed** or **The investment produced no useful result.**

## IELTS transfer task

Write a short four-sentence response about an IELTS topic such as education, health, transport, technology, or the environment. Use at least two examples of the target grammar from this chapter. Underline those examples, then check that every sentence has a clear subject, a correct verb, and a meaning that is easy to understand.
