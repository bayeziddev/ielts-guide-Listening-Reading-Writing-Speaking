# 10. Conditional Sentence

> **Chapter focus:** zero, first, second, third, mixed, and alternative conditional forms.

## How to study this lesson

Read the explanation first, then say the examples aloud. Change one detail in each example, such as the subject, time, or place. Complete the practice without looking back and use the answer key to understand the reason for each answer. Finish by writing two sentences about your own life or city.

A conditional sentence links a condition with a result. The **if-clause** gives the condition and the main clause gives the result. The clauses can change order: **If governments invest, services improve** and **Services improve if governments invest**. A comma is normally used when the if-clause comes first.

### Main conditional patterns

**Zero conditional** describes general truths: **If water reaches 100°C, it boils.** Use present simple in both clauses.

**First conditional** describes a real or possible future result: **If the government invests in rail, congestion will decrease.** Use present simple after *if*, not *will*: **If the policy succeeds**, not usually **If the policy will succeed**.

**Second conditional** describes an unlikely or imaginary present/future situation: **If I were the mayor, I would improve bus services.** In formal English, **were** can be used with all subjects in the phrase **If I were…**.

**Third conditional** describes an unreal past and its imagined result: **If the city had planned better, it would have avoided the problem.** The condition uses *had + past participle*; the result uses *would have + past participle*.

**Mixed conditionals** connect different times: **If the city had invested earlier, it would have better transport now.** Use them only when the time relationship is clear.

Other expressions include **unless** (*if not*), **provided that**, **as long as**, and **even if**. **Unless** already contains a negative meaning: **Unless the plan changes, costs will rise**; do not write **unless it does not change** when you mean the same thing.

### IELTS use

Conditionals are useful for recommendations and consequences in Task 2: **If schools teach media literacy, young people may evaluate online claims more carefully.** Do not force them into every paragraph. A correct first conditional is more valuable than a complex mixed conditional used incorrectly. [2]

### Practice

Complete the sentences: (a) If people used less plastic, the environment ___ (benefit). (b) If I ___ (be) responsible, I would fund public libraries. (c) If the company had checked the data, it ___ (avoid) the error.

**Answers:** (a) **would benefit** for an imaginary situation, or **will benefit** if the writer means a realistic future condition; (b) **were**; (c) **would have avoided**.
